CoreWeave

An AI boom is easier to forecast than to value

Our team valued CoreWeave using discounted cash flow, public comparables, and precedent transactions. The methods disagreed substantially. Why?

Abstract

In an April 2025 team valuation, we combined discounted cash flow, public comparables and precedent transactions for a capital-intensive AI infrastructure business. The methods produced widely different estimates: a $10-per-share base DCF and a $22 synthesis across methods. The analysis makes the dependence on financing, investment and distant cash flows visible rather than treating a single estimate as definitive.

Separate the valuation methods

The base DCF uses a 12.2% discount rate and 2.6% terminal growth. Public comparables and precedent transactions supply different reference points, with differences in company selection and transaction context.

Why the spread matters

Capital expenditure, working capital and long-run cash generation determine how revenue growth converts into equity value. Segment-based comparable adjustments introduce additional judgment. The estimates below are selected central outputs from distinct methods, not confidence bounds.
