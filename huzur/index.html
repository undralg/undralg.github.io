Abstract

After college graduation, my friends scattered across different countries. Our regular card games became considerably harder to organize! I wanted to keep playing Huzur, a popular household card game in Mongolia, but couldn’t find a website where we could play together. So I built one. The platform supports two to five players, online multiplayer lobbies, and an AI opponent I'm continuously improving.

A playable system and an evaluation loop

Players can create accounts, join lobbies and play local human/AI matches. Cloudflare Workers and D1 support persistent accounts, match state and records. Decision snapshots and exports connect the application to subsequent analysis.

Information boundaries shape the AI

The opponent combines strategic heuristics, lookahead and exact two-player endgame search. Public-information guards restrict the decision inputs; self-play supports comparisons between policies. Strong simulated play alone does not establish performance against human opponents.

What remains to build

The documented roadmap includes server-side rules validation, realtime transport and personalized policies. The current implementation is a working platform with an evaluation foundation, rather than a completed competitive multiplayer service.
