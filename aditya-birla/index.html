Aditya Birla

Two investments look profitable. What has to go right?

For Aditya Birla’s proposed international investments, project profits were only part of the story. I adjusted cash flows for ownership and remittances to value what the parent company would receive, then examined how much depended on terminal value.

Abstract

I developed two international capital-allocation proposals: an India–UAE SME payments business and a low-carbon cement investment. Parent-level DCF models yield positive base-case NPVs for both, but terminal value accounts for a substantial share of their economics. The analysis distinguishes attractive forecast outputs from the execution and remittance assumptions needed to produce them.

Value cash received by the parent

The forecasts adjust for ownership and remittances rather than equating project cash generation with parent cash receipts. Payments assumes a 70% ownership share; the cement proposal applies a 90% remittance assumption.

Compare scale and dependence on the terminal period

The payments proposal requires 70 crore of initial investment and produces a modeled NPV of 190.5 crore. Cement requires 375 crore and produces 485.1 crore. Discounted terminal values of 195.51 and 617.3 crore respectively show how strongly continuation assumptions influence the result.

Decision under uncertainty

These are proposed investments, not implemented projects. A decision would require testing volumes, margins, cross-border execution and terminal assumptions, including scenarios where growth or cash remittance falls short.
