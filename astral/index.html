Astral Records

A profitable investment. An uncomfortable financing problem.

Astral Records’ equipment investment had a small positive modeled NPV and a much larger funding requirement. I examined whether the company could finance the decision.

Abstract

The Astral Records case combines declining margins with a growth plan and an equipment decision. Under the forecast, adding a $1 million machine raises two-year financing needs from roughly $741,000 to $1.7 million. Its incremental NPV is only about $10,600 under the working-capital assumptions, making financing capacity and sensitivity central to the decision.

Forecast growth through the balance sheet

Operating margin fell from 24.7% in 1991 to 17.1% in 1993. The forecast assumes 15% sales growth, but receivables, inventory, debt servicing and capital expenditure determine how much external financing that growth requires.

Compare purchase timing, not purchase versus nothing

The capital-budgeting comparison evaluates buying the machine now against replacement later. Its small positive incremental NPV depends on working-capital benefits; it should not be read as an unconditional case for immediate investment.

Funding and value answer different questions

A positive NPV does not ensure the firm can comfortably finance the project. The case places the incremental valuation alongside the much larger funding requirement.
