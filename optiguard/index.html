Optiguard

Same exit. Different fine print. Millions apart.

Two financing structures can divide the same exit proceeds very differently. I modeled Optiguard’s capitalization and payout waterfalls to quantify how liquidation preferences and antidilution terms change what investors and founders receive.

Abstract

I modeled how antidilution and liquidation preferences change venture-financing outcomes beyond the headline valuation. In a $15 million exit scenario with an option reserve, the investor receives approximately $6.15 million under a conventional preference versus $9.52 million under a participating preference. The economic difference comes from contractual rights, not a change in the exit value.

Model the capitalization before comparing terms

The analysis starts with a $5.7 million Series A investment at $4 per share, then considers a hypothetical $7.8 million round at $3. Weighted-average antidilution adjusts conversion terms; it does not simply guarantee an unchanged ownership percentage.

Follow the payout waterfall

A conventional preference requires comparing the preference payout with conversion. A participating preference adds participation in residual proceeds. At the same exit value, that distinction materially reallocates proceeds between investors and founders.

Keep the scenario explicit

The figures depend on the modeled reserve, capitalization and exit assumptions. They do not describe an executed investment or a guaranteed recovery.
