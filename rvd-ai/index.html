How does the size of an initial investment change the ownership bargain?

Abstract

Using the venture-capital method, I examined how a larger initial investment changes the ownership required by an investor. At the report’s fixed $4.33 million post-money estimate, raising $2.5 million implies a 57.7% investor stake; raising $3.5 million implies 80.8%. The analysis exposes the dilution embedded in the growth and exit assumptions.

Work backward from an exit scenario

The model assumes a $1.49 billion addressable market, 2% market share, a 10% earnings margin, an exit P/E of eleven and a 50% required return over five years. These assumptions produce the valuation used for both funding comparisons.

Hold valuation constant to isolate the funding tradeoff

The larger raise provides another $1 million but reduces the residual founder share from approximately 42.3% to 19.2%. These are simplified ownership calculations before any subsequent rounds or additional dilution.

What the calculation does not establish

Market size is not revenue, and assumed market share is not demonstrated traction. The ownership outcomes are scenario results rather than negotiated financing terms.
