Toyota: financial policy across borders

How do production location, liquidity and currency exposure shape financial policy?

Historical course case study

Abstract

This historical case examines how Toyota’s financial policy relates to production geography, liquidity and currency exposure. Peer ratios place Toyota’s 2011 consolidated debt-to-assets ratio at 63%, but differences in financing subsidiaries limit a simple leverage ranking. The analysis links financial capacity to the operating structure behind the ratios.

Start with comparable definitions

The report compares automakers in the 2010–2011 case setting. Consolidated balance sheets include both manufacturing and financial services, so debt supports different activities across firms.

Connect the balance sheet to operations

Domestic production creates different currency and cost exposures from production closer to regional demand. The case considers geographic flexibility alongside liquidity and leverage, rather than treating financial ratios as an isolated scorecard.

Interpret the peer comparison narrowly

The chart describes consolidated accounting ratios in the source report. It does not establish that a company with lower consolidated debt necessarily has lower manufacturing risk or a superior current financial policy.
