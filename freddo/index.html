Freddo: growth under inflation

How can an expanding business manage cash when inflation and shipping complicate the cycle?

Historical course case study

Abstract

This expansion case connects a marketing proposal with working-capital discipline. The operating model uses a 70-day cash conversion cycle; reducing inventory from 60 to 30 days would lower it to 40 days if receivables and payables remain unchanged. The exercise makes explicit which operational changes the expansion economics would need to support.

Translate operations into cash tied up

The baseline cash cycle is 20 receivable days plus 60 inventory days minus ten payable days. The target changes inventory rather than assuming all three components improve simultaneously.

Separate the operating target from the valuation

The campaign model reports an NPV of approximately $72,015 under its assumptions. The original valuation uses the 70-day cycle; the 40-day figure is a proposed operational target, not an improvement already included or achieved.

What would need validation

Conversion, cannibalization, shipping costs and local production feasibility drive the business case. The projected benefits are estimates for a course proposal, not measured savings or realized expansion results.
