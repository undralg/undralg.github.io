Vinyl’s opportunity doesn’t end at release week.

Streaming made buying a physical album optional. Yet vinyl grew while CDs declined. What explains the difference—and what does opening week miss?

The central finding

Older recordings take a larger share of vinyl purchases.

National growth does not necessarily mean vinyl wins at launch. Historical market breakdowns show that established recordings account for more of vinyl purchases than CD purchases.

This points to a sales opportunity beyond the release calendar. It does not establish that every album ages better on vinyl, or explain buyers’ motives.

From a market puzzle to an album-level question.

01 / MARKET

Start with RIAA

Compare annual CD and vinyl units, revenues and revenue per copy. Test whether relative affordability offers a simple explanation for their divergence.

02 / LAUNCH

Build a sales dataset

Assemble 201 publicly reported album-format observations across 132 albums. Compare first-week sales, then examine 41 matched albums with advertised prices.

03 / BEYOND LAUNCH

Follow the question

Use exploratory album histories to ask what happens later, then examine broader catalog breakdowns to see how the age mix differs by format.

The opening-week regressions were inconclusive. Public reporting creates availability bias, and the album histories are a small exploratory sample. The memo separates those clues from the stronger descriptive market evidence.
