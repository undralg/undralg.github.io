Wrigley: the price of leverage

Does a $3 billion debt-funded payout create value once financial distress enters the picture?

Historical course case study

Capital structureAPVWACCSensitivity analysis
Read analysisOpen financial model

Abstract

I evaluated a $3 billion debt-funded payout by separating the tax shield from borrowing costs and expected financial distress. The case’s $1.2 billion no-distress benefit falls to $732 million in the base distress scenario. A smaller $1.83 billion recapitalization offers less modeled value but stronger interest coverage, illustrating why the largest modeled payout need not be the preferred financing policy.

Value the financing benefit and its cost

The APV analysis adds the debt tax shield and subtracts expected distress costs. Changing the assumed loss conditional on distress reduces modeled net value from $732 million to $576 million. These are scenario estimates, not observed shareholder gains.

Debt capacity constrains the recommendation

At $3 billion of debt, modeled interest coverage is about 1.2 times. The proposed $1.83 billion alternative raises coverage to roughly 2.2 times and produces about $520 million of net value under the case assumptions.

Do not confuse EPS with value

A repurchase raises modeled EPS relative to a dividend by reducing shares outstanding. That denominator change does not itself create enterprise value. The analysis also considers how tender participation changes family voting control.
