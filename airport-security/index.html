More security lanes. Same bottleneck?

Airport queues do not behave like independent service stations when they share a senior screening officer. I implemented a discrete-event simulation and compared it with queueing approximations to examine the hidden bottleneck.

Abstract

This team project models airport security as a system of interacting queues. I implemented a discrete-event simulation with shortest-queue routing and a shared senior officer for secondary screening, then compared simulated performance with queueing approximations. The comparison exposes why adding primary stations alone may not remove congestion created by a shared resource.

Model the shared resource explicitly

Passengers arrive at ten per minute and choose the shortest queue. Primary screening takes about thirty seconds on average, while secondary screening introduces another dependency. A shared officer couples the stations, so treating extra screening as independent service time can miss the bottleneck.

Compare simulation with theory

The notebook tests simplified queueing approximations against simulated waiting times and queue lengths. The report then summarizes eighty runs of the main scenario, showing how congestion falls as station count increases.

A recommendation needs a decision rule

The report favors eight stations as a practical compromise between waiting and utilization. That is a judgment under the scenario, not a cost-optimal staffing result: labor costs, service targets, transient behavior, and the secondary-screening design would need further analysis.
