The approach

Analyzed job-training outcomes using experimental and observational data, propensity-score and genetic matching, and instrumental variables. A second application uses synthetic control and placebo diagnostics to examine California’s 2011–2015 drought and crime.

What the analysis shows

The job-training exercise illustrates why treatment selection matters: a naive observational comparison can reverse the direction of the randomized comparison. Matching is assessed against the experimental benchmark; the drought analysis makes the counterfactual and placebo evidence explicit.

How to read the results

These are course analyses, not new randomized experiments. Causal interpretations depend on balance, identification assumptions and the credibility of the comparison group.
